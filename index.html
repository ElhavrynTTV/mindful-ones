Sound Safety & Responsible Use

Listening should feel gentle and be easy to stop. This section explains how to listen safely, what this site can and cannot do, and how we keep science and tradition honestly labeled.

🦻 Hearing protection

  • Always begin at a low volume and increase slowly only if it stays comfortable.
  • Avoid prolonged listening at high volume, and take regular breaks.
  • Stop listening and rest your ears if you notice discomfort, pain, ringing (tinnitus), muffled hearing, dizziness, or headache.
  • If ringing or hearing changes persist, seek advice from a qualified hearing professional.

🔉 A safe starting volume

The generator now starts at a conservative 20%, with a smooth fade-in when you press Play and a smooth fade-out when you press Stop or the timer ends, to reduce sudden sounds and clicks.

No slider percentage guarantees a safe listening level. The same 20% can be quiet on one phone and loud through amplified headphones on another. Your device volume, headphone type, and surroundings all matter — when in doubt, turn it down.

🎧 Headphones & binaural beats

Traditional binaural-beat playback requires stereo headphones or earbuds. It works by sending a slightly different frequency to the left ear and the right ear; your brain perceives the difference as a beat.

On speakers, the two tones mix in the air before reaching your ears, so you hear a blended tone rather than a true binaural beat. Check that your headphones are in stereo and worn on the correct ears.

♿ Accessibility

Controls are built to be usable by keyboard, with visible labels, readable type, and good contrast in light and dark modes. Animations respect your device's prefers-reduced-motion setting. If any control is hard to use on your device, listening is always optional — every tone also has a text description of what it is.

⏹️ Audio stops reliably

  • One generator at a time: starting a new tone first stops the previous oscillators, so tones do not stack and get louder.
  • Stop: fades out and disconnects all oscillators in both ears.
  • Timer: uses two stop paths — a page timer and a stop scheduled on the audio clock itself — so expiry stops playback even if the tab is backgrounded.
  • Errors: if audio fails to start, playback state is reset so the Play button does not get stuck showing a tone that is not playing.

📱 Background listening & the lock screen

Mindful Ones runs as a web page, and your browser and phone — not this page — decide whether web audio may keep sounding after you press Home or lock the screen. Many browsers suspend the audio engine in the background: the tone goes silent, but the session is paused rather than cancelled.

What we have observed so far: testing on Android inside the Muse app, the tone becomes inaudible on Home or lock, the session timer keeps counting down, and the tone is audible again when the app is reopened before the timer ends. In Android Chrome, outside Muse, all three background checks passed: audio continued through headphones after pressing Home, audio continued with the screen locked, and after the one-minute timer expired, playback stopped and remained stopped when returning to the page. That strongly suggests the earlier background-audio limitation was associated with Muse's embedded viewing environment rather than an inherent limitation of the sound generator, although the exact cause has not been technically proven. Background behavior can still vary by browser, device, and settings, so we do not promise uninterrupted background playback on every setup.

Whatever the browser does, these safety rules hold: a suspended tone never restarts louder than you set it, tones never stack, and a session never runs past its timer — the stop is scheduled on the audio clock itself. The diagnostics readout in the Frequency Studio shows the audio engine's live state (running / suspended / interrupted) so you can see what your device is doing.

Implementation notes that can affect those tests

  • How tones are made: the Web Audio API generates the exact Hz value shown (e.g. an oscillator set to 528). A spectrum app held near a speaker measures your device's output, room, and microphone as well as the tone — small readings off the exact number usually come from the measuring setup, not the setting.
  • Binaural channels: on modern browsers each ear is routed with a StereoPanner (−1 left, +1 right). Very old browsers fall back to a channel merger. A channel-accurate test should record left and right separately, not just listen.
  • Timer: two mechanisms run together — a page countdown that calls Stop, and a stop scheduled directly on the audio clock. The audio-clock stop is there because browsers throttle page timers in background tabs.
  • Background & lock screen: browsers may pause, throttle, or suspend web audio when a tab is backgrounded or a phone locks, and the behavior differs between browsers and apps. Observed inside Muse on Android: audio goes silent in the background while the timer keeps counting — suspension, not cancellation. In Android Chrome, outside Muse, all three background tests passed (Home, lock screen, and timer expiration stopping and staying stopped). That strongly suggests the earlier limitation was associated with Muse's embedded viewing environment rather than an inherent limitation of the sound generator, although the exact cause has not been technically proven. This page still does not promise uninterrupted background playback on every browser or device. The diagnostics readout in the Studio shows the audio engine's live state while you test.
  • Rapid actions: every Play, preset change, or mode switch first disconnects the previous oscillators, then starts fresh ones with a fade-in. If you hear any doubling or click during rapid switching, note the exact steps — that is exactly what the error-handling check is for.
  • Try it yourself: play 528 Hz with an analyzer app nearby; in Binaural mode cover one ear then the other; set the 1-minute timer and let it expire; slide volume to 0% while playing. Repeat on your phone as well as your computer.
